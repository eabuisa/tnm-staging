T categories

Clinical

CategoryDefinition
TXPrimary tumor cannot be assessed / cannot be identified.
T0No evidence of a primary tumor.
TisCarcinoma in situ.
T1Tumor less than 2 cm in greatest dimension.
T2Tumor 2 cm or more but less than 4 cm in greatest dimension.
T3Tumor 4 cm or more in greatest dimension, OR minor bone erosion, OR perineural invasion, OR deep invasion. Perineural invasion means tumor in the sheath of a nerve 0.1 mm or larger in caliber, a nerve lying deeper than the dermis, or clinical/radiographic involvement of a named nerve without skull base invasion. Deep invasion means invasion deeper than the subcutaneous fat, or more than 6 mm thick measured from the granular layer of adjacent normal epidermis to the tumor base.

T4bTumor with skull base invasion and/or skull base foramen involvement.

Notes

This AJCC 8 chapter applies ONLY to cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma of the head and neck; cutaneous SCC at other body sites has no AJCC stage grouping. Clinical and pathologic N categories DIFFER because extranodal extension (ENE) is judged clinically (overt ENE on examination) for cN and microscopically after neck dissection for pN: any clinically overt ENE+ is cN3b, whereas pathologically a single node 3 cm or less with ENE+ is only pN2a, and larger or multiple ENE+ nodes are pN3b. The stage groups themselves are the same for clinical and pathologic staging, so there is no separate stageGroupsPathologic. IMPORTANT: in the AJCC 8 cutaneous carcinoma of the head and neck chapter the stage groups are 0, I, II, III, and a single Stage IV (per the NCI PDQ table reprinted from the AJCC 8 manual) - Stage IV is NOT subdivided into IVA/IVB/IVC in this chapter. Some reviews present an AJCC/UICC combined table that splits Stage IV into IVA (N2/N3 or T4, M0) and IVB (M1); that subdivision is the UICC presentation and is not used here. T0 (no primary found) with nodal disease is not enumerated in the published stage-group table and is excluded from the coverage check.

Reference only — staging must be confirmed against the full AJCC 8th edition chapter and the patient's complete clinical picture.
