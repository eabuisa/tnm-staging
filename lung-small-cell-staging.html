T categories

Clinical

CategoryDefinition
TXPrimary tumor cannot be assessed, or cancer cells are found in sputum or bronchial washings but the tumor cannot be seen on imaging or bronchoscopy.
T0No evidence of a primary tumor.
TisCarcinoma in situ: squamous cell carcinoma in situ, or adenocarcinoma in situ (a pure lepidic, non-invasive tumor 3 cm or smaller).
T1miMinimally invasive adenocarcinoma: tumor 3 cm or smaller, predominantly lepidic, with an invasive component of 5 mm (0.5 cm) or smaller.
T1aTumor 1 cm or smaller in greatest dimension. Also includes a superficial spreading tumor of any size that is confined to the bronchial wall.
T1bTumor more than 1 cm but no more than 2 cm in greatest dimension.
T1cTumor more than 2 cm but no more than 3 cm in greatest dimension.
T2aTumor more than 3 cm but no more than 4 cm. A tumor 3 cm or smaller is also T2a if it involves a main bronchus without involving the carina, invades the visceral pleura, or is associated with atelectasis or obstructive pneumonitis extending to the hilar region.
T2bTumor more than 4 cm but no more than 5 cm in greatest dimension (with or without the T2 features described under T2a).
T3Tumor more than 5 cm but no more than 7 cm; or invading the parietal pleura, chest wall, phrenic nerve, or parietal pericardium; or separate tumor nodule(s) in the same lobe as the primary tumor.
T4Tumor more than 7 cm; or invading the diaphragm, mediastinum, heart, great vessels, trachea, carina, recurrent laryngeal nerve, esophagus, a vertebral body, or the inner (visceral) pericardium; or separate tumor nodule(s) in a different lobe of the same lung.

Notes

AJCC 8 applies the same TNM categories and stage groups to small cell lung cancer as to non-small cell lung cancer, so the tables here are identical to the NSCLC tables. In practice, small cell lung cancer is also commonly described with the older Veterans Administration Lung Study Group (VALG) two-stage framework: limited-stage disease can be encompassed within a single tolerable radiation field, while extensive-stage disease extends beyond that (including distant metastases). The same T, N, and M descriptors are used for clinical and pathologic staging, so the lists are identical. For part-solid tumors the T size is based on the invasive component; superior sulcus (Pancoast) tumors are T3 unless they invade the vertebral body/spinal canal, encase the subclavian vessels, or involve the C8 or higher brachial plexus roots, which makes them T4.

Reference only — staging must be confirmed against the full AJCC 8th edition chapter and the patient's complete clinical picture.
