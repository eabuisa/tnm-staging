Penis — TNM Staging (AJCC 8th)

T, N and M category definitions and stage groups for penis from the AJCC 8th edition.

T categories

Clinical

CategoryDefinition
TXPrimary tumor cannot be assessed.
T0No evidence of a primary tumor.
TisCarcinoma in situ (penile intraepithelial neoplasia, PeIN).
TaNoninvasive localized squamous cell carcinoma (in the 7th edition this was called noninvasive verrucous carcinoma).
T1Tumor invades the tissue just under the surface (subepithelial connective tissue / lamina propria) (parent category; see T1a and T1b).
T1aSubepithelial connective tissue invasion WITHOUT lymphovascular invasion, WITHOUT perineural invasion, and NOT poorly differentiated (grade 1-2).
T1bSubepithelial connective tissue invasion WITH lymphovascular invasion or perineural invasion, or the tumor is poorly differentiated (grade 3 or sarcomatoid).
T2Tumor invades the corpus spongiosum, with or without invasion of the urethra.
T3Tumor invades the corpus cavernosum (including its tunica albuginea), with or without invasion of the urethra.
T4Tumor invades other neighboring structures, such as the scrotum, prostate, or pubic bone.

N categories

Clinical

CategoryDefinition
NXRegional lymph nodes cannot be assessed.
N0No palpable or visibly enlarged inguinal lymph nodes.
N1One palpable, mobile inguinal lymph node on one side.
N2Palpable, mobile inguinal lymph nodes that are multiple or on both sides.
N3A palpable fixed inguinal nodal mass, or pelvic lymph node enlargement on one or both sides.

Pathologic

CategoryDefinition
NXRegional lymph nodes cannot be assessed.
N0No regional lymph node metastasis.
N1Metastasis in one or two inguinal lymph nodes on one side, without extranodal extension.
N2Metastasis in three or more inguinal lymph nodes on one side, or in inguinal nodes on both sides.
N3Metastasis in a pelvic lymph node (one or both sides), or tumor growing outside a lymph node (extranodal extension) in a regional node metastasis.

Notes

AJCC 8th edition separated corpus spongiosum (T2) from corpus cavernosum (T3) invasion and removed urethral invasion from the T2/T3 distinction. Stage groups are the same for clinical and pathologic staging; only the N definitions differ between the two lists.
