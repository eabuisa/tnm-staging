T categories

Clinical

CategoryDefinition
TXPrimary tumor cannot be assessed.
T0No evidence of a primary tumor.
Tis (DCIS)Ductal carcinoma in situ — abnormal cells confined to the ducts, no invasion.
Tis (Paget)Paget disease of the nipple with no invasive cancer and no DCIS in the underlying breast tissue.
T1Tumor 20 mm or smaller in greatest dimension.
T1miMicroinvasion: tumor 1 mm or smaller in greatest dimension.
T1aTumor more than 1 mm but no more than 5 mm in greatest dimension. (Measurements just above 1 mm, up to 1.9 mm, are rounded to 2 mm so they are not misclassified as microinvasion.)
T1bTumor more than 5 mm but no more than 10 mm in greatest dimension.
T1cTumor more than 10 mm but no more than 20 mm in greatest dimension.
T2Tumor more than 20 mm but no more than 50 mm in greatest dimension.
T3Tumor more than 50 mm in greatest dimension.
T4Tumor of any size growing directly into the chest wall and/or the skin (ulceration or visible skin nodules). Invasion of the dermis alone does not qualify as T4.
T4aExtension into the chest wall. Sticking to or invading the pectoralis muscle alone, without chest wall invasion, does not qualify.
T4bSkin ulceration, and/or visible satellite tumor nodules in the skin of the same breast, and/or skin swelling (edema, including peau d'orange) that does not meet criteria for inflammatory carcinoma.
T4cBoth T4a (chest wall) and T4b (skin) features are present.
T4dInflammatory carcinoma — a clinical diagnosis with rapid redness, swelling, and warmth involving at least one-third of the breast skin, usually with cancer cells in the skin lymphatics on biopsy.

N categories

Clinical

CategoryDefinition
NXRegional lymph nodes cannot be assessed (for example, they were previously removed).
N0No spread to regional lymph nodes on examination or imaging.
N1Spread to movable level I/II armpit (axillary) lymph nodes on the same side as the tumor.
N1miMicrometastases in regional nodes — deposits larger than 0.2 mm but none larger than 2.0 mm (usually found on biopsy before surgery).
N2Spread to same-side level I/II axillary nodes that are fixed to each other or to other structures (matted), or to internal mammary nodes alone.
N2aSpread to same-side level I/II axillary nodes that are clinically fixed or matted.
N2bSpread to same-side internal mammary (along the breastbone) nodes, with no clinical involvement of the axillary nodes.
N3Spread to infraclavicular nodes, or to internal mammary plus axillary nodes, or to supraclavicular nodes on the same side.
N3aSpread to same-side nodes below the collarbone (infraclavicular / level III axillary nodes).
N3bSpread to same-side internal mammary nodes together with level I/II axillary nodes.
N3cSpread to same-side nodes above the collarbone (supraclavicular nodes).

Pathologic

CategoryDefinition
NXRegional lymph nodes cannot be assessed (for example, not removed, or previously removed).
N0No spread to regional lymph nodes on microscopic examination, or isolated tumor cells only.
N0(i+)Isolated tumor cells only — tiny malignant cell clusters no larger than 0.2 mm in regional nodes. Counted as node-negative.
N0(mol+)Positive molecular test (RT-PCR) in a regional node, but no tumor cells or isolated tumor cells seen. Counted as node-negative.
N1Micrometastases, or spread to 1–3 axillary nodes, and/or spread found by sentinel node biopsy in internal mammary nodes that were not clinically enlarged.
N1miMicrometastases only — deposits larger than 0.2 mm but none larger than 2.0 mm.
N1aSpread to 1–3 axillary lymph nodes, with at least one deposit larger than 2.0 mm.
N1bSpread (bigger than isolated tumor cells) to same-side internal mammary sentinel nodes.
N1cBoth N1a (1–3 axillary nodes) and N1b (internal mammary sentinel node) are present.
N2Spread to 4–9 axillary nodes, or to clinically detected internal mammary nodes with negative axillary nodes.
N2aSpread to 4–9 axillary lymph nodes, with at least one deposit larger than 2.0 mm.
N2bSpread to same-side internal mammary nodes seen on imaging, with pathologically negative axillary nodes.
N3Spread to 10 or more axillary nodes, or infraclavicular nodes, or internal mammary plus axillary involvement as defined in N3b, or supraclavicular nodes.
N3aSpread to 10 or more axillary lymph nodes (at least one deposit larger than 2.0 mm), or to infraclavicular (level III axillary) nodes.
N3bClinically detected (imaging-positive) internal mammary nodes together with positive axillary nodes (N1a/N2a pattern), or more than 3 positive axillary nodes together with a positive internal mammary sentinel node that was clinically negative.
N3cSpread to same-side supraclavicular lymph nodes.

M categories

Clinical

CategoryDefinition
M0No clinical or imaging evidence of distant metastasis.
M0(i+)No clinical or imaging evidence of distant metastasis, but tumor cells or deposits no larger than 0.2 mm are found by microscopic or molecular tests in circulating blood, bone marrow, or other non-regional nodal tissue in a patient with no signs or symptoms of metastasis. Grouped as M0 for staging.
M1Distant metastasis found by clinical examination or imaging (cM1), or proven under the microscope in a distant organ or in non-regional nodes with deposits larger than 0.2 mm (pM1).

Pathologic

CategoryDefinition
M0No distant metastasis. (Pathologic M is usually taken from the clinical assessment unless a distant site has been biopsied.)
M1Distant metastasis, histologically proven in a distant organ or as deposits larger than 0.2 mm in non-regional lymph nodes (pM1).

Notes

Two staging bases — use the Anatomic / Prognostic toggle above. Anatomic staging uses T, N, and M alone. Prognostic staging, the AJCC-preferred basis where biomarker testing is routine (including in the US), uses the AJCC prognostic stage tables, which combine T, N, and M with histologic grade and ER, PR, and HER2 status — so the same TNM can land in a different prognostic group depending on tumor biology. The clinical and pathological prognostic tables are not identical; the app applies the one matching the Clinical / Pathologic toggle. The Oncotype DX rule (recurrence score < 11 → prognostic stage IA for T1–T2, N0, M0, ER-positive, HER2-negative tumors) is part of pathological prognostic staging only. Tumor size is the greatest dimension of the largest invasive focus; sizes are not added for multifocal tumors. cM0(i+) (tumor cells found only by microscopic or molecular tests in blood, bone marrow, or non-regional nodal tissue, with no clinical or imaging evidence of metastasis) is grouped as M0. Lobular carcinoma in situ (LCIS) is not staged in the 8th edition.

Reference only — staging must be confirmed against the full AJCC 8th edition chapter and the patient's complete clinical picture.
